# Position relative to controlled hedge removal

Nnamdi et al. already edit hedges under experimental control. Appendix F.2 compares their certainty metric with ROUGE-L under de-hedging and unrelated content removal. J.7 varies the fraction of hedges removed from clinical documents. J.8 uses a judge explicitly prompted to assess the direction of certainty change. K and N cover rater agreement and supervised distortion detection. This corrects the earlier shorthand that the study only measured generation and only compared metrics of overlap. The presence of an explicit certainty instruction, the hedge manipulation, and a directional judgment each have precedents. [Nnamdi et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2026.aacl-long.223.pdf)

The question retained for P10 concerns default binary support decisions, with the same edited scientific claim judged under its source sentence and full abstract. P10 requires an annotator to determine that the stronger claim remains unsupported by the full abstract before either model decision enters the primary contrast. The strength-instruction comparison additionally measures costs on copied and weakened claims. A full abstract can legitimately change the correct verdict. This motivates a human warrant label for each focal edit.

The November 19 search of ICLR 2027 titles and abstracts is documented in the Gate 0 report. A targeted November 24 web-index update for arXiv and OpenReview using grounding, verifier, scientific claims, certainty, hedges, and partial support did not establish an identical design. The web search is not an exhaustive review. The manuscript makes no priority claim. The natural science-communication dataset remains outside the current sample. [Wührl et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.369/)
